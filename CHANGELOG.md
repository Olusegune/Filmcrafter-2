## 1.30.0 (2026-10-09)

- **Choose your picture model and video model in the wizard** (optional, under *How it renders*), and in Plan & budget. Each model shows its real price, whether a real render has proved it, whether a picture model can see your character pictures, and a plain blurb: what it is good for, when to consider it, what to keep in mind. Choosing one fixes it for the project, which keeps characters consistent; the advice to use **one picture model for the whole project** is shown, and Plan & budget now warns when a project's keyframes come from more than one picture model and none is pinned.
- **Better handling of hands and anatomy.** *Prevent:* people in a shot add a line asking for correct anatomy (stronger when hands hold, touch or work), and models with a negative prompt are told to avoid extra, missing and fused fingers. *Flag:* the keyframe check and the take review now look at hands and limbs as a problem of their own (**Hands and body**), counting fingers and checking where hands hold each other, with a severity: clearly wrong hands fail a keyframe, soft ones pass with a "look at the hands" note. *Fix:* a failed keyframe is re-made once with a standard correction (five distinct fingers, one hand in front of the other); a flagged take carries it into Fix. This lowers the odds; it cannot remove them, and the checks need a vision-capable AI brain.
- **Retry a failed render on a cheaper model.** A failed render in the Render queue offers up to three cheaper, proven models with their prices; one click (after a price confirmation) queues the shot again. Never for content-filter refusals, key or credit problems, or renders that may already be billed; end-frame shots only go to models that take one; pictures keep their model. **Retry failed videos on a cheaper model automatically** (Plan & budget, off by default) does it by itself, once per shot, only for failures during the current render, only to a cheaper model, and inside the budget.
- Tests: blurbs for every model family, the picker list and the mixed-model warning (`test_model_choice.py`, `modelchoice.test.tsx`); the anatomy guard, negative prompt, prompt placement, checker schemas and verdict rules, and the keyframe check wording (`test_anatomy.py`, `keyframecheck.test.tsx`); what is offered, what is never offered, sending, once only, budget refusal, the automatic path and its limits (`test_retrycheap.py`, `cheaperretry.test.tsx`).

## 1.28.0 (2026-10-09)

- **Undo for changes to a shot.** Approving a take or keyframes, choosing a keyframe, changing a shot's start frame, end frame, motion or take prompt, and locking or unlocking a shot are recorded, and a note offers **Undo** right after. `Ctrl+Shift+U` undoes the latest (and again to go further back), `Ctrl+Shift+Y` redoes, and the command palette names the next one (*Undo: Approve take · S01-04*). The library and trash indexes now also refuse any entry whose file name is not a bare name.

## 1.20.0 (2026-10-08)

- **Start → End frames, first class.** Every shot's inspector has a **Start → End** tab (and `E` opens it for the shot under the playhead). Two big slots, a three-step guide (start frame, end frame, render) that always says what is next, and one **Render start → end** button that shows the price first. Bring a picture from Midjourney, Photoshop or anywhere by pressing **Use my image…** or by **dropping it on either side**; the start slot also continues from the previous shot's last frame or remakes the keyframe. End frames can be your own, the next shot's start (the two shots flow with no cut), or left to the model. Ideas for how a shot ends are one tap away, transformations first ("the same pose, now decades older"). The tab gets a dot when the shot has an end frame.
- **The Lighting Designer.** *Light the story* (rail button, command palette, or the link in a shot's Camera & lighting) reads the mood of every shot, from the Director's feeling, the sung words, the action, the performance intensity, and how loud the song is at that moment, across 17 moods. It plans the light for each: the kind of light, its direction, colour temperature, contrast, accent colour, practical lamps, haze and how the light moves. A scene keeps one light unless the mood really turns; night scenes are never lit as day; loud parts get more contrast and tender or grieving ones stay soft. A colour strip shows the film's emotional arc; apply to all, or only the shots you tick; lights you chose yourself are left alone unless you ask; one press of **Undo** puts everything back. Pictures you make in Midjourney itself work everywhere by dropping them on a shot's start or end frame.
- **Dance check fixed:** a damaged or absurd tempo in a song map (a billion BPM, for one) could make the Dance check build a grid of a billion beats and never return. Impossible tempos now mean "tempo unknown" and the check returns at once.
- Tests: the frames stage (own start image, locked shots, bad files), the mood reader, light design, planning, applying, undoing, the strip colours, the Midjourney request and polling shapes, the damaged song maps.

## 1.19.0 (2026-10-08)

- **Extend this take.** When a shot is longer than the video model can make in one go, the last seconds only hold the final frame. A new card on the shot's Video tab says so (shot 12 s, take 8 s) and offers **Extend by 4 s**: it renders a continuation that starts on the take's last frame (same people, set and light, no cut, action carried forward), shows the price first, and joins the two into one longer take. The old take is untouched and stays in your video until you swap the longer one in (Review → New versions ready, or Takes → Use this one). Works from the video under a lip-synced take; the joined take is lip-synced again afterwards. If the model's longest clip still leaves it short, it says so and you can extend again.

This is the golden path for a one-shot transformation: start frame, end frame, then extend only if the shot is longer than the model's longest clip.
- If the join fails, the billed clip is kept and the take says so, in plain words.
- Tests: the advice and its numbers, the last frame, joining clips of different sizes and rates (real ffmpeg), the whole path with a mock provider, the failure path, the card's wording (`test_extend.py`, `extend.test.tsx`).

- **The Production Bible goes into prompts as plain visual rules.** Markdown headings, the document title and notes meant for the editor ("Text to add in editing", SVG resources, insert cards) are left out, which saves room and stops invitations to paint written words into the picture.
- **Fixed an absurd fal.ai price** (a 4-second Seedance 1.5 Pro shot showed "$103,680"): a price quoted per million tokens was being applied per token.
