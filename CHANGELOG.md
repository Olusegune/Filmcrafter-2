# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Compositing for layers (Unified Studio, milestone 5, first slice).** The Layers panel has a new **Mix and effects** section (and a layer's glow and blur, built earlier, now have their sliders there). **Mix with the picture** gives a layer a blend mode (multiply, screen, overlay, soft light, hard light, darken, lighten, add, difference); the finished film does the arithmetic in full-range RGB with the HD matrix written out, only while the layer is up, and the rest of the film is left as it was; the player shows it too. **Show only through a shape** (oval, rounded box, rectangle, with a size and a soft edge, or turned inside out for a vignette) hides the rest of the layer. On a picture layer, **Cut out a colour** removes a screen colour even in shadow (it compares which colour it is, not how bright), with a soft edge and a slider that takes the screen's colour off the edges of what is kept. Free and local. Not built yet: keying or blending video clips (layers are still pictures, text and shapes), animated or drawn masks, and tracking.

- **Polish my film.** When every required step is done, the line under the film's header ('Everything is in place. The film is ready to export.') gains a **Polish my film** button. It opens the Studio and walks through the three finishing tools one after another: the cut suggestions, then colour, then titles. Closing a sheet moves on to the next; a small bar says what is left and has **Stop here**. Nothing is applied except what you accept in each sheet, and every one can be undone.

- **The Guide now leads to the new tools.** Besides the path from story to export, the **Guide** lists four optional, free steps: **Start from a preset** (offered before anything is made), **Tighten the cut**, **Colour**, and **Titles and lower thirds**. Each one's *Take me there* gets the film ready and opens the Studio with that sheet already up, so there is nothing to hunt for in the rail or the menus. They never hold up the path: a film with every required step done is still ready to export.

- **Motion graphics (Unified Studio, milestone 4, first slice).** A **Motion graphics** sheet (the Layers menu's *Motion graphics…*, the timeline's *+ Layer* menu, or Ctrl+K) offers eight ready-made titles: lower third, title card, chapter title, place and time, callout, quote, big number and end card. Choose one, type its words (each has one or two boxes, up to 60 characters), see a picture of it drawn by the same code that draws it in the film, and press **Add at the playhead**. It becomes a few ordinary layers in one group, so you can still change the words, colours, timing and movement in the timeline and the Layers panel, and **Take it away again** removes just that group. Layers also gained **glow** (a soft halo in a colour you choose) and **blur**, drawn into the layer's own picture, kept in saved looks and templates, and unchanged for every existing layer. Free: nothing is generated and no provider is called. Not built yet: blend modes, masks, parent and child layers, motion paths, and building motion from a written request.

- **A restyled shot now says how the people themselves are built in that look.** The characters kept coming out smooth and glossy in Low-poly (and other looks) because the reference pictures show them that way and a model follows a picture before words. 24 of the most surface-distinct looks (low-poly, voxel, clay, plasticine, paper, brick, vinyl toy, felt, pixel art, flat vector, cel-shaded, hand-drawn, watercolour, comic, woodcut, ink, pencil, stained glass, mosaic, collage, cut-out, stop-motion CGI) now carry a plain description of how faces, hair and clothes look in that look, put early in the prompt, plus what they must not look like in the negative prompt. Other looks get a general rule. Not checked with a real render: if a look still comes out too smooth, the next step is a character sheet drawn once in that look and used as the reference.
- **Presets (Unified Studio, creative preset engine, first slice).** A **Presets** button on the rail (and in Ctrl+K) opens a searchable library of 35 presets, using the thumbnails from your preset pack: the pack's 18 (Vertical Micro-Drama, TikTok Story Short, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, Animated Short, Historical Storybook, Product Commercial, Documentary, four micro-dramas, five short-form kinds, Cinematic Storybook 2.5D) and the layer presets they stack (seven storybook looks, three acting styles, three editing styles, two colour grades, three delivery versions). Add one or more to a **stack**: included presets go first, a later preset wins over an earlier one, and **a choice you already made beats them all**. **Preview what this changes** lists every setting as *New*, *Already so*, *Yours, kept* (with a *Replace mine* tick) or *Not available*, and says that nothing is regenerated, no provider is called and your film keeps its own shape; **Apply** changes only settings (the film's look, its acting profile, its colour grade, its editing style, its delivery, its length to aim for, and the frame shape for pictures made from now on, which is again yours once pictures exist); **Undo the last application** puts back only what is still as it left it. Favourites, recently used, **Keep this as my own preset**, and **save as a file / add a preset file** (settings only: no paths, pictures, keys or LUT files; a bad or newer file is refused with a reason). Presets are data checked on load; a preset cannot run anything.
- **A vertical version that never touches the original, and a free micro-drama series plan.** The 9:16 export already made a separate file; now each shot can keep a chosen part of the wide picture (left, middle, right: **Presets, Vertical version**), and in a vertical version captions are kept clear of the bottom margin the delivery preset asks for (the original, its records and its exports are unchanged, proven by comparing frames). **Series plan** makes an editable plan for a season of short episodes (10 by default, 60, 90 or 120 seconds each, any number from 1 to 60) from an idea, using the people and places already in the film: for each episode a hook, an escalation, a turning point, an ending, continuity notes, a cliffhanger switch and an optional budget cap, with a shape that opens, builds, turns in the middle, hits a low point and closes. Every suggestion is marked *Suggestion* until you write or keep it (*Yours*); it is a scaffold, not a story written for you, and a cliffhanger in every episode is flagged as formula. **Check the plan** reports what is still a suggestion, who appears late, and the budgets you set; **save as a document**. Free: nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is charged.
- **The Smart Editor (Unified Studio, milestone 3, first operations).** A **Suggest…** button on the cut lane (and *Smart Editor* in Ctrl+K) opens suggestions for the cut. Each says **what** it would change, **why** it may help, **which clips** it touches and how much time it saves or adds, can be **previewed as a real before and after** (the film's own export of that part), and changes nothing until you press **Accept**; accepting is one change, one Ctrl+Z takes it back, and **Not this one** keeps it away for good (until you ask for the rejected ones back). Two rules are built, both free, local and deterministic: **tighten** (a clip with spoken lines that runs well past the time the words need: end it a little after the last word) and **give a listener more time** (a clip of someone listening straight after another person spoke, when the take has more picture or is a still). The editor **never trims a pause you asked for**: a beat you wrote that is still playing, *Hold the reaction longer*, or *More hesitant* switched on leaves the clip alone, and the sheet lists what it left alone and why (a draft that you never kept is not read). Three editing styles are built (*Restrained character drama*, *Intimate conversation*, *Rhythmic montage*); the other seven of the ten are listed as not built yet and cannot be chosen. You can also type a request ("tighten this scene", "give the audience more time on her reaction", "punchy"); it is read by keywords, says what it understood, and says plainly when it does not (continuity, transitions and camera movement are not built) instead of guessing. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is charged.
- **Preview copies of big clips, and a damaged edit record is never read as 'never edited' (Unified Studio, milestone 2, step d).** Under the cut lane's **More** menu, **Make preview copies of big clips** makes a small (540 lines) quick-to-scrub copy of every clip that is taller than 720 lines or larger than 60 MB, kept in the project's cache and used only by the Studio's player; the finished film (and Preview) always read the take itself, which a test proves by comparing the exported frames with and without copies. **Remove preview copies** deletes only those files. A change of take gets a new copy. Separately, a review of crash recovery found that a damaged edit record would have been read as an empty one and quietly turned the film back into its plan: now a damaged record is put back from its backup (and you are told, the damaged bytes are kept), and with no good backup everything stops with a plain message and nothing is written over it. (A separate recovery journal was not needed: every edit is already saved atomically with a backup.)
- **The cut lane in the timeline's Edit mode (Unified Studio, milestone 2, step c).** Edit mode of a film now has a **Cut** lane above the shots: every clip of the film's cut with trim handles (drag an edge; hold Alt on the end handle to roll the cut), drag a clip to move it, a marker flag, and a toolbar with **Undo, Redo, Split, Delete** and a **More** menu (ripple delete, trim to the playhead, close the gap, marker, lock the track, and the **Ripple** and **Snap** switches). Keys: **S** cuts at the playhead, **Delete** and **Shift+Delete** (ripple) take the chosen clip out, **Q** and **W** trim its start or end to the playhead, **B** puts a marker, **Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Shift+Z** undo and redo the cut, a grade or a saved look (the Layers panel keeps its own when it is open), and **Alt+arrows** nudge a clip a frame (**Shift**: a second); the same things can all be done with the keyboard alone. Dragged edges snap to other cuts, markers and the playhead. The lane says plainly when a shot is not in the cut (with a *Place it* button), when a clip's shot left the plan, and when a layer now starts after the end of the cut. The Film strip view shows none of it. **The rest of the Studio follows the cut:** the shot cards, the transition handles, the voice and planned-sound lanes, the shot count, the player (it shows the right moment of a trimmed take), selecting a shot (it goes to where the shot is now) and the next/previous shot keys all use where each shot sits in the cut. A shot that is not in the cut is not shown as a card. Free layers and captions stay where they were put (the lane tells you when one now starts after the end).
- **The film's cut can be edited (Unified Studio, milestone 2, first two steps; the controls come next).** The film now has a real cut, kept in the film (`edit/edit.json`) in exact whole-number time (254,016,000,000 ticks a second, so every common frame rate is a whole number of ticks and no edit can drift by a frame). Behind the new `editing` door: cut a clip in two, trim its start or end (regular, or ripple so everything after it follows), delete (or ripple delete), move (or ripple-insert between clips), roll the cut between two clips, close a gap, place a shot that has no clip, markers, named ranges, lock/hide/mute a track, snapping to cuts and markers, and **one undo and redo that covers a cut, a grade and a saved look alike**. A film that was never edited is read from the shot plan exactly as before and nothing is written by opening it; the first edit stores the cut once. A shot added to the plan afterwards is *unplaced* and a clip whose shot was taken out is an *orphan*; the export leaves such clips out and says so, never silently. The export applies trims frame-exactly (checked against frame-numbered test footage), a shot cut in two does not dissolve into itself, and recorded voices, the shot's own sound and planned sound cues keep their place in a trimmed clip. The edit record is schema 2; a record from a newer FilmCrafter is refused, not written over. The hand-off files (EDL, XML, Premiere, Resolve) say the cut is not in them yet. There are no on-screen controls for these edits yet (milestone 2, step c).
- **Color Studio (Unified Studio, milestone 1).** A new **Color** sheet (rail, under *More*; Ctrl+K) puts a grade on the whole film, a scene or one shot: exposure, brightness, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, temperature, tint, saturation, vibrance, hue, lift, gamma, gain, and red, green and blue separately. It shows a before and after frame made by the same filter text the export uses, says plainly when a grade pushes the picture past white or into pure black, saves your own looks, and can undo the last save. **`.cube` LUTs** can be imported (strictly checked, copied into the film by content hash), labelled creative or technical with the colour space each expects and makes, and applied with an intensity. FilmCrafter's pictures are Rec.709, so a LUT for log, HDR or another space is kept but refused with a reason, never guessed. The grade lives in the film (`edit/edit.json`), not in the render project, so making the render project again cannot lose it; the colour match (the cast fix) still comes first, and the takes are never changed. Free and local.
- **Cinematic Storybook 2.5D, with six variations, and Style DNA.** A look can now be described as structured parts (character design, proportions, faces, line and outline, surface, palette, shadows, light, materials, backgrounds, depth, camera, animation, motion, expression, finish, and what to avoid) and the wording is made from them. The new looks (*Cinematic Storybook 2.5D*; *Storybook: historical*, *epic illustration*, *modern urban*, *folktale illustration*, *fantasy*, *educational documentary*) are written from the general visual grammar of the owner's own reference pictures, not from any picture or character, and are visual variations, not historical reconstructions. The reference pictures are not part of the product.
- **Performance Director, steps three to five.** On the **People** tab you can say what *kind* of character someone is (a person, a stylised person, an animal, a robot, a creature); only what you choose is used, and the prompt then names what can carry their feeling (ears and posture for an animal, head tilt and pauses for a robot) instead of a human face, worded for the film's look. Shots about lifting, running or pulling, and about a hug or a handshake between two people, get a short effort or contact line from the shot's own words. The **Emotional arc** section now points at places worth a look (a sudden swing, a stall, a long stretch at full strength, a drop with no step) and each can be dismissed. Behind the scenes there is an honest model label (*tested*, *documented*, *unverified*; acting quality is never called verified) and a delivery hint for a line taken from beats you wrote. All free and local.
- **Old music-video code removed.** The song-first screens that a film could never reach (three directions from a song, the Golden Path strip, the welcome tour, the song materials panel, the test-shot picker, Direct My Video, tempo and singer fields) and their engine and shell commands are gone. Films, their timing and captions are unchanged.
- **Performance Director (first two steps).** The **Acting** sheet now reads each scene and drafts what it is for, what each person wants and hides, and which spoken lines may mean more than they say. Drafts are labelled **Draft** and never reach a prompt; press **Keep this** or write your own and it becomes **Yours** (the only words that are used). **People** holds what a person is like (goal, fears, how stress shows), beside what the cast already says. On a shot, **Suggest beats (free)** gives a rule-based starting point, beats can carry eyes, head, posture and what changes, and nine one-tap directions (more restrained, less exaggerated, more vulnerable, hold longer, ...) are added to that shot only. Nothing here calls a provider or costs anything; the engine refuses to save beats or arcs that were not confirmed.
- **Prompt practice from the CINEDANCE block library.** A restyled shot now also says which rule matters most and what makes the shot wrong ("if the characters look like they belong to a different style from the background, the shot is wrong"), and tells the model to take from the reference pictures only who the people are and what the place contains, not their lighting, colour grade, depth of field, framing or rendering. "More restrained" (strong) now uses calibrated substitutions (a shout becomes a quiet exhale, shock a small recoil before any expression) and "Let the eyes tell the story" adds real gaze behaviour (micro-movements, irregular blinks, head and eyes agree, nobody looks into the lens).
- **A look for one shot now reaches the characters, and lives on the Keyframe tab.** Low-poly (or any look) had been applied to a tree but not to the girl under it: the prompt told the model to keep her skin, face, hair and clothes *identical* to the reference pictures, which are drawn in the film's look, and a model follows a picture before words. A shot or scene in a look of its own is now restyled as a whole: the people keep who they are but are drawn in the new look, the reference pictures are tagged "take what it shows, not how it is drawn", and the shot before is used fully when it is in the same look and only for layout when it is not. A shot in the film's own look is written exactly as before. New option **Keep the characters as they look in the film** (only the world changes). The control moved from the Shot tab to the Keyframe tab, where the picture is made; nothing renders when you change it and the price is still shown first. *Not yet seen with a real model.*
- **A calmer first run (a UX pass).**
  - **Connections is grouped by what the keys are for:** *Pictures and video* (you need one: Kie.ai is the easiest start; fal, Google and BytePlus the others), *Writing helper and picture checks*, *Voices*. Each row keeps the same shape whether a key is saved or not, and "Not connected" no longer wraps onto two lines; Remove is a small link next to Connected.
  - **The left rail shows the path** (Home, Project, Library, Studio, Looks, Review, Export) and keeps Supervisor, Acting, Continuity, Layers, Lighting, Colour match and Clean up behind one **More** button (remembered; Director Mode always shows them). Review and Export now come before the advanced tools.
  - **Fixed: the Studio's top-bar icons covered the inspector's title** ("Project", "Shot S01-04"). The inspector's header now leaves exactly as much room as the bar spills over it, at any window width, and Ask the Director moves into More when the bar is tight.
  - **Fixed: the Interface size dropdown in Settings had no name** for a screen reader (a critical accessibility fault the new scan found).
- **BytePlus ModelArk: Seedream direct from ByteDance.** A new picture provider (Settings > BytePlus key): Seedream 5.0 Pro (automatic), 5.0 Flash, 5.0 Lite, 4.5 and 4.0, asked for at the film's shape and the size the quality tier maps to, with references sent in the request as base64. BytePlus publishes its prices, so the price sheet is exact: Pro $0.045 (to 1.5K) or $0.09 (2K), plus $0.003 for each reference after the first; Flash $0.018, Lite $0.035, 4.5 $0.04, 4.0 $0.03. 4K is on 4.5, 5.0 Lite and 4.0 (Seedream 5.0 Pro stops at 2K everywhere). Every render still needs the confirmation and the budget check; the key goes only in the credential store and never in a message. *Not yet proved by a real picture.*
- **Fixed: a price in fractions of a cent was shown rounded down.** A 4.5-cent picture was shown (and counted by the budget) as 4 cents; sums are now rounded up to the cent and BytePlus spend is kept to a hundredth of a cent.
- **Seedream 5.0 Pro (and 4.5) at their largest size.** *High* and *Max* quality now ask a picture model for the largest size it offers, in the film's shape: Seedream 5.0 Pro 2K (Kie: `quality` high; fal: an exact 2048-pixel long edge), Seedream 4.5 4K. Draft and Standard keep the basic size. Seedream 5.0 Pro is a recommended card on fal as well as Kie, and the picker says *up to 2K* or *up to 4K* for the Seedream models (the sizes the providers publish; not yet proved by a real render here). - **Fixed: a draft could ask for 4K.** A Kie picture model that lists 1K, 2K and 4K (Nano Banana Pro) was asked for 4K at every quality, Draft included, while the price shown was the learned price of a smaller picture. Draft and Standard now ask for the smallest, High and Max for the largest.
- **A look for one shot, or one scene.** A film has one look, but a dream or a memory can now be played in another. On the **Shot** tab, *Look for this shot* (first choice: *Same as its scene (or film)*), or the **Look** chip in the shot's header, which opens the look picker for that shot alone. A shot's own look wins over its scene's, which wins over the film's. ## 0.31.0 (2026-10-27)

- **The hand-off is read back before you are told it is done.** After the folder is written it is opened the way an editor would: every file a timeline points at must be there, no track may play two sounds at once or run past the film, the Final Cut XML must be well-formed with every clip defined, and the EDL must have one event for each shot with a take. Anything wrong is said in a sentence under the result; a clean folder says so. (It also found a real problem on a real film: very long titles made folder paths too long for Windows, so names are now kept short.)
- **Each track as one finished file.** The hand-off folder now has `audio/stems`: one stereo WAV per track (each voice, effects, ambience, music), as long as the film, so they line up from zero in any editor. This is also the way into After Effects.
- **When each voice falls (Sound plan, second tab).** For every shot with a voice: a strip that shows the picture, the J cut before it, the L cut after it, and each person's voice where the film's mix lays it. A voice with no recording yet is hatched, with its length estimated from its words. A shot whose voices run past its end is marked and says by how much. **Play the voices** reads a shot's recordings in order, a table read.
- **The video price beside the voices.** The Sound plan now says what making the video for the shots with people speaking would cost with the model Auto would use (including the lip-sync pass where one runs), from the app's own price tables and what that model has cost before. A shot with no price is counted as not priced and the figure is called a minimum. Nothing is spent from there.
- **Engine dependency advisories fixed.** Pillow 13.0.0 and pypdf 6.21.2 replace versions with newly published advisories (the whole engine test suite passes on them).
- **Sound on tracks for your editor.** The hand-off folder's OpenTimelineIO and Final Cut XML now carry each person's voice on a track of its own (Voice Sarah, Voice John...), and the approved sound effects, ambience and music on theirs, every clip at its real frame: the same place, J and L cuts, gaps and levels the exported film is mixed with. A looping sound is laid end to end to fill its time. The sounds are copied into `audio/voice` and `audio/cues`. For Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve, **Send to your editor** adds a third timeline, *shots with sound by track*, with the same shots over one audio track per voice and per kind of sound; the first two timelines are unchanged. Not in the EDL (it cannot carry this much).
- **The next step, always in view.** A slim line under the film's header says the one next thing to do (a fix first, then cast pictures, voices, keyframes, video, approval, export), with a button that goes there. **All N things** opens **Is this film ready?**: everything open, in the order a film is made, each with the reason and a button. A shot that is too short for its line can be **lengthened to fit in one click**, and put back. It only reads: a paid step sends you to Make, where the price is shown first.

## 0.29.0 (2026-10-26)

- **Depth of field, chosen like an aperture.** On a shot's **Camera** tab, **Depth of field** offers *Very shallow (about f/1.4: only the eyes are sharp), Shallow (about f/2.8), Medium (about f/5.6)* and *Deep (about f/11: everything sharp)*. The request says it in a cinematographer's terms. Left on **Automatic**, shots about people get a softly out-of-focus background and wide shots are left alone; a choice you make stands in place of the automatic one.
- **J and L cuts for a shot's dialogue.** On the **Voice** tab of a shot with dialogue, **The line starts before the picture** (a J cut: you hear them before you see them) and **The line carries on over the next picture** (an L cut), 0 to 2 seconds each. They are used when the film is exported: the recording is not changed and the picture stays where it is. A lead can never push a line before the start of the film.

- **Composition is now built in.** Every picture and video request for a shot about people now says where they sit in the frame, worked out from the shot size, who is in it and which way the blocking plan has them facing: one person on a third with look room in front of the face and their eyes on the upper third line; two people on opposite thirds facing in; a horizon on a third and layered depth in a wide shot; the foreground shoulder and the opposite third in an over-the-shoulder shot; the thing they look at in a point-of-view shot. It is left out when a storyboard panel already sets the framing, and one switch turns it off for the film. On a shot's **Camera** tab, a **Composition** box draws the thirds grid with the points marked and shows the sentences that go into the request. In the pictures screens, **Thirds grid** draws the rule-of-thirds lines over the pictures so you can judge where people sit.
- **Pacing report** (Shots screen, **Pacing...**). Reads the cuts as they are and reports the rhythm scene by scene (a bar for every shot, tall for long) and where it works against the story: a shot too short for its own dialogue (a fault, with the length it needs), very short or very long shots, four of the same shot size in a row, identical shot lengths, a scene that does not open wide, a long exchange with no close-ups, a long speech in one shot, a scene much faster or slower than the rest, and transition effects on most cuts. Each finding has a reason, a suggestion and a button to its shot. It only reads.

## 0.28.4 (2026-10-22)

- **Messy, changing backgrounds.** Found by reading the picture's own record: the playground was only ever called "Lincoln Middle School - Playground - Day", with no description, no layout and no picture, so the picture model invented the fence, the tables and the building again in every shot; nine references went with it (four character sheets each for two people, and the previous shot) and none for the place. Four changes:
  - **A heads-up above the pictures** when a place that shots happen in has no description, layout or picture, with **Fill in from the story (free)** (it writes the story's own words about the place, never over a description that exists) and a button to open Places in Cast & World.
  - **A soft background for shots about people** (not wide or establishing views, and not when you chose the focus yourself): the request now asks for a gently out-of-focus background, which also hides what image models smear.
  - **Fine repeating patterns kept soft** in every request (fences, mesh, brick, leaves, grass: never sharp, noisy or moire), and the same words in the negative prompt for models that take one. ## 0.2.0 (2026-10-10)

The first version that takes a film from a screenplay to an exported file.

- **Story:** a screenplay (or an idea, synopsis, treatment or outline) is kept exactly as given; scenes, speaking characters and places are read from it; versions; Story Lock; suggestions that never edit the script.
- **Production Bible, Cast and World:** characters, places and props with related states, wardrobe, your own pictures, place layouts, rules and a visual style; filled from the story, then yours; every change is kept in a history.
- **Shots:** the story broken down into a draft of shots, each a Shot Contract (purpose, people and states, place, action, dialogue, acting beats, camera, light, continuity); suggestions with reasons; split, join, duplicate, move, take out.
- **Making the film:** storyboard panels, hero keyframes and video through the proven render engine, with choose and lock at each stage, your own pictures and video, a soundtrack, and export. Every generation shows its price and waits for a yes.
- **Continuity Director:** advisory findings ("may have changed") with reasons; your decisions are kept.
- **Hand-off:** EDL, OpenTimelineIO, Final Cut Pro XML, shot manifest, subtitles, copied takes and soundtrack.
- **Simple and Pro** modes on the same film; **Director Command** (Ctrl+K) for plain phrases about a shot.
- New app icon from the FilmCrafter mark.
- Tests: 90 new engine tests including a whole film from screenplay to export with no provider and no spend.
