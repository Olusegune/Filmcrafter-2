# Phase 2: Continuity Director (29 Sep 2026)

## What was built

The **Continuity** sheet has its own button in the left rail. It works per scene.

- **Blocking.** For each person, you set a screen side (left, middle or right) and which way they face.
  - The defaults put the two leads facing each other across the line. Everything can be edited.
  - A small diagram shows the stage from the camera's side.
- **180° rule and eyelines.** Every keyframe and video prompt in the scene now says:
  - who stands where;
  - that the camera stays on one side of the line between the leads;
  - which way each person looks: toward their partner, or toward where the partner is when they are off-screen.
- **Wardrobe, props, light and time of day.** These are taken from the scene's script notes and first shots, can be edited, and are carried into shots that don't state them themselves.
- **The previous shot as a reference.** When a keyframe is made, the scene's previous approved keyframe is sent last in the reference list, labelled "match its set, light, wardrobe and props, not its framing". It is the first reference dropped when a model accepts only a few.
- **First/last-frame continuity (free).** In the shot panel, *Start from the end of the previous shot* takes the last frame of the previous take and makes it this shot's keyframe. A *Continuous* flag per shot marks cuts that continue in time.
- **Cross-shot review:**
  - **Free rules.** These flag:
    - jump cuts (two shots of the same size and the same people back to back);
    - people missing from the blocking;
    - people with no reference image;
    - continuous shots that aren't chained yet.
  - **AI review.** The AI makes one pass per scene over its frames. It reports breaks in wardrobe, hair, props, lighting, time of day, screen direction or eyeline, the axis, the set and identity. It says which shot breaks and how to fix it, and each finding is shown on that shot.
- **Opt-in by design.** Nothing changes in a project's prompts until its Continuity sheet is opened (or a check is run). A scene can be switched off.

- **Tests:** 116 engine tests pass, 6 of them new. They cover blocking defaults surviving user edits, the prompt wording, jump cuts, the previous-keyframe reference only after setup, and a real ffmpeg last-frame chain checked by pixel colour.
- **Installed app, Exit Plan copy:**
  - Blocking was derived for all 9 scenes; the free rules found jump cuts.
  - The AI review of all 9 scenes (9 calls to gpt-5.5, a few cents) found 27 breaks. Examples:

    - the suitcase missing in S01-07;
    - screen sides swapped in S01-08, S02-02, S03-03, S04-04 and S08-03;
    - the gate design changing in S03-10;
    - a lighting change in S07-10.
  - The free prompt preview for S01-08 now carries the blocking, the 180° rule and the eyelines, plus the previous-shot reference.
- **Installed app, BYOM Test:** S04-02 was chained from the last frame of S04-01's clip.

## Known limitations

- Blocking is a plain left, middle or right with a facing direction. There is no top-down floor plan or camera positions yet.
- The AI review judges keyframes (or your stills), not motion inside video takes.
- The default blocking is a starting point from who appears first. Screen-direction findings are measured against it, so check the blocking before acting on them.
- Nothing is re-made automatically; fixes are your call (and cost).

## Gate

Continuity works end to end on Windows:

set up → prompts carry it → references inherit → chain shots → review → persisted in `continuity.json`.
